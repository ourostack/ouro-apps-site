Ouro Shepherd · Experimental

Keep agents on task.

Agents often hand control back before they need to. Shepherd watches the agents in your terminals and judges each of those stops. If an agent stopped too early, Shepherd answers it in the terminal, where you can see it. If it needs you, Shepherd tells you.

The problem

Agents stop too early.

Run a few agents at once and a pattern shows up. One asks "want me to go ahead?" about work you already asked for. One offers a menu where the right choice is obvious. One writes a plan and then waits. Each stop sits there until you notice it, and you lose the time in between.

Shepherd answers those stops for you, in the open, so your attention goes to the ones that are really yours.

  • Fewer stalls you have to find yourself
  • Every answer shown where it happened
  • Real decisions still come to you

What it does

A second look at every stop.

Shepherd is part of Ouro, our open-source agent harness. Here's what it does. It follows the terminal's own signal that an agent has stopped, then reads the screen to decide what kind of stop it was.

Judges every stop

Each time an agent hands control back, a quick model call decides whether it should have.

Answers in plain view

A reply is typed into the terminal as [Ouro for <your name>], and that terminal's status line says what was answered and why. A menu gets a key press instead, recorded on the status line and in the log.

Leaves background work alone

An agent waiting on its own subagents hasn't really stopped. Shepherd won't answer it and logs the stop as progress.

Stays out of your terminal

It never types into the terminal you're using, and it checks the screen again just before it sends anything.

Knows when to back off

After three answers in a row to one agent with no sign of you, it stops answering there and tells you.

Fixes the cause

Every decision is logged. When the same kind of early stop keeps happening, your Ouro agent will propose a fix to the instructions that caused it.

How it decides

Trust the agent. Show the work.

Ouro works on a simple idea we call "trust the fucking agent": an agent doing the job you gave it should keep going, not stop to ask permission. So Shepherd has no allowlist of approved commands. That includes permission prompts: if the work is routine and inside the task, Shepherd may press the key that approves it.

What keeps that accountable is visibility. Every answer appears in the terminal and on its status line, and every decision goes into a log on your machine that you can read.

  1. The agent stopscmux or Herdr reports that the agent has finished its turn. If it starts again within a few seconds, Shepherd lets it be.
  2. Shepherd reads the screenA judge reads the last lines of the terminal and decides: stopped too early, needs you, finished, or still waiting on its own work.
  3. It acts on thatToo early: it answers. Needs you or finished: it tells you. Still working: it leaves the agent alone.
  4. It writes it downEvery decision goes into the log, with the reason.

If the judge can't tell, or fails, Shepherd does nothing. The agent's question stays in the terminal for you.

What comes to you

Some things will always come to you.

Shepherd is built to leave these alone. It leaves the question in the terminal and your Ouro agent tells you about it, on Telegram for now. Finished work comes to you the same way. The judge is a model and can be wrong, which is why every answer it does give is visible and logged.

Your voice

Anything that would be sent or published as you.

Your decisions

Choices that are yours to make, not the agent's.

Your money

Purchases, payments, and anything that spends for you.

Your credentials

Sign-ins, passwords, keys, and accounts only you can act in.

Irreversible acts

Anything that can't be undone.

Real ambiguity

When it isn't clear what you meant or whether the agent may do it.

Where it stands

Experimental, inside Ouroboros.

Shepherd ships in Ouroboros, our open-source agent harness, as an early alpha. We use it every day, and some of its newest safety rules don't cover every path yet, so treat it as an experiment and turn it off to stop it.

You'll need an Ouro agent running on the same computer as your terminals. The judge runs on your agent's model provider, so each stop it judges is a small model call on your account. On your phone, decisions arrive on Telegram; for the terminal itself, use Heeler with Herdr, or SSH over Tailscale.

Turn it on Ouroboros on GitHub

Your terminal, your agents

Shepherd isn't a terminal and isn't a fork you have to install. It connects to stock cmux through its socket (you store a token once, from a cmux terminal) and to Herdr through its local socket. No cmux account needed.

It is built to be agent-agnostic. It uses the terminal's own stop signal and reads the screen, so no part of it depends on one particular agent. We're testing it with Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot CLI and Agency.
