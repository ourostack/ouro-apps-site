Ouro Shepherd · In development

Keep every coding agent moving.

Shepherd lets an Ouro agent watch the Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Copilot CLI sessions you run in cmux. It tells you who needs you, answers the routine prompts the way you would, and brings you everything else in one digest.

The problem

More agents, same one of you.

Running several coding agents at once is fast until you become the bottleneck. You tab through terminals to find the one that's stuck. You approve the same harmless command for the tenth time. You come back from lunch and can't tell what each session did.

Shepherd will sit beside your sessions and do that triage for you, so your attention goes to the decisions that are actually yours.

  • One answer to "who needs me right now?"
  • A plain summary of what each session did
  • One message instead of a stream of pings

What it does

Triage for your coding agents.

Shepherd will follow your sessions through cmux's own agent events and screen text, then do the small jobs you'd otherwise do by hand.

Who needs you

One answer across every workspace: which sessions are waiting on you, and for what.

What each session did

Ask about any session and get a short account of what it did and where it stands.

A status line per workspace

A one-line status on each cmux workspace, so the sidebar shows where each one stands.

One digest

Shepherd will wait for things to settle, then send one summary instead of a ping per event.

On your phone

If your Ouro agent can message you, for example over iMessage, it sends the digest there too.

Routine prompts, answered

Permission prompts you'd approve anyway will be answered for you, with a one-line reason you can check.

How it decides

Your judgment, not a rulebook.

Shepherd won't grow a long list of rules. It will work from a short set of principles you agree with and a casebook of decisions you actually made. When you answer a prompt it escalated, or tell it "you should have asked me", that will become a new case.

Principles will change only when you approve the change. Every answer will be logged with its reasoning, so you'll always be able to see why it did what it did.

  1. Safety floorPlain code, no model. Anything on the floor will go straight to you.
  2. Your precedentIf you've decided this exact kind of prompt before, it will follow your decision.
  3. Your principlesFor something new, it will reason from your principles and closest cases, and answer only when it's confident and the floor agrees.
  4. Ask youOtherwise it will hand the prompt to you, with its best guess attached.

If it can't decide in time and you don't answer, nothing is approved. The prompt stays in cmux for you.

The safety floor

Some things will always come to you.

The floor will be plain code, checked before and after any model judgment. Nothing a model says will be able to move it.

Credentials

Passwords, tokens, keys, and two-factor codes.

Destructive operations

Deleting files, force pushes, hard resets on shared branches.

Deploys

Production deploys and tearing down infrastructure.

Money and terms

Purchases, and accepting terms on your behalf.

Your voice

Anything that would be sent or published as you.

Outside the repo

Anything that reaches beyond the project the session is working in.

Where it stands

In development, built in the open.

There's nothing to download yet. Shepherd is being built as part of Ouro, our open-source agent harness. This page will say so when you can try it.

You'll need an Ouro agent running on the same Mac. Shepherd's judgment calls use your agent's model provider.

Follow along on GitHub

Built for stock cmux

Shepherd isn't a new terminal and isn't a fork you have to install. It's built for stock cmux, the open-source macOS terminal, and will work through cmux's documented command-line and socket interface (you turn on socket automation once). No cmux account needed.
